\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Rigid-body flow matching reduces molecular-crystal structure prediction to a lattice,
fractional centroids, and per-molecule orientations on $\mathrm{SO}(3)$, but the current
generation of rigid-body molecular-crystal generators leaves crystallographic symmetry
unconditioned, and symmetry-conditioned generation has been demonstrated only for inorganic
atomic sites. We show that the per-molecule orientation target factorizes as
$R_m=\mathrm{rot}(g_m)\,R_{\text{asym}}$ into a space-group-determined relative rotation, which a
flow learns, and a gauge-free asymmetric-unit pose, which it cannot regress from packing. We turn
this into a deployable method: a leak-free coset label---the generating space-group operation,
recoverable from a symmetry template at sampling time---conditions the orientation flow.
On molecular crystals from the Cambridge Structural Database, deployable coset conditioning lifts
the held-out non-reference orientation loss by $41.1\%$ (vs $27.5\%$ for a paired no-coset
control), two-thirds of the way to a leaky-codebook oracle ($\sim\!48\%$); an
$\mathrm{SO}(3)$-averaged objective closes the gap ($47.7\%$). Supplying the coset at generation
collapses the end-to-end orientation error $7\times$ ($93.5^\circ\!\to\!13.4^\circ$). For
template-free use, a packing-only predictor places the true coset in its top-5 for $85.6\%$ of
molecules, so marginalizing the top-$k$ recovers most of the benefit without a template.
At $2.2\times$ scale the coset advantage \emph{widens} (loss drop $+41.1\%\!\to\!+54.3\%$ over a
$+36.8\%$ control) and the coset-supplied orientation error sharpens to $4.3^\circ$, while exact
match stays lattice/centroid-limited. Scored in MolCrystalFlow's own units, the coset supplies the
orientation conditioning that symmetry-free rigid-body flows lack (\S\ref{sec:bench}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================
\todo{Expand from the workshop intro (paper/submissions/MoML/main.tex \S1).} Crystal structure
prediction (CSP) seeks the stable periodic packing of a chemical system; generative models have
become a fast alternative to large density-functional
ensembles\cite{xie2022cdvae,jiao2023diffcsp,miller2024flowmm}, and Riemannian flow
matching\cite{lipman2023flowmatching,chen2024riemannian} traverses a straight conditional path in
tens of steps. Rigid-body factorization is attractive for molecular crystals: it collapses a
molecule to a lattice $L$, a fractional centroid $c_m\in\mathbb{T}^3$, and an orientation
$R_m\in\mathrm{SO}(3)$.

\paragraph{The gap.} Recent rigid-body molecular-crystal flows leave space-group symmetry
\emph{unconditioned}: MolCrystalFlow\cite{zeng2026molcrystalflow} uses the identical
$\text{lattice}\times\mathbb{T}^3\times\mathrm{SO}(3)$ factorization but imposes no space-group
constraint, and MOFFlow\cite{kim2025mofflow} relies on framework connectivity. Symmetry- and
Wyckoff-conditioned generation is proven only for inorganic atomic
sites\cite{jiao2024diffcsppp,wyckoffdiff2025,symmcd2025,mu2026nextcrystal}; none conditions
rigid-body molecular orientation. We close that gap and make the conditioning \emph{deployable}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.05em
\item A leak-free, template-available coset label (the generating operation
$h=g_mg_0^{-1}$, from centroids alone) conditioning the orientation flow.
\item Deployable conditioning recovers two-thirds of the oracle-codebook orientation benefit;
an $\mathrm{SO}(3)$-averaged objective closes the rest.
\item End-to-end, the coset collapses the orientation error $7\times$.
\item A template-free path: a packing-only predictor whose top-$k$ covers the true coset for
$86\%$ of molecules at $k{=}5$, made usable by top-$k$ marginalization.
\item The coset advantage \emph{widens} with data (\S\ref{sec:scale}; $2.2\times$ scale: gate
$+41\%\!\to\!+54\%$, orientation-isolated match $27.5\%\!\to\!56.8\%$), while exact match stays
lattice/centroid-limited. In MolCrystalFlow's own units (\S\ref{sec:bench}) our diagnostic-scale
generator is packing-limited end-to-end, but supplies the orientation lever the symmetry-free flows
omit.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Rigid-body flow}
\todo{From MoML \S2 + diagnostic Methods.} A molecular crystal is a lattice $L$ and, per molecule,
$(c_m,R_m)$ acting on a shared body-frame conformer, $\text{cart}_{m,a}=c_mL+R_m\text{local}_{m,a}$.
Conditional flow matching\cite{lipman2023flowmatching} is trained jointly over a volume-normalized
lattice, torus centroids, and $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ orientations, with an $\mathrm{E}(n)$-equivariant
encoder\cite{satorras2021egnn} and pair-bias attention, conditioned on the space group.

\subsection{The orientation decomposition}
$R_m=\mathrm{rot}(g_m)\,R_{\text{asym}}$; the free gauge on $R_{\text{asym}}$ floors the absolute
orientation head at $\mathbb{E}\lVert u_R\rVert^2\!\approx\!5.24$, while re-gauging to the relative
$R'_m=R_mR_0^{-1}$ exposes a learnable factor (loss drops $27\%$). \todo{full derivation from the
archived diagnostic (main-archive-2026-07-18-diagnostic.tex \S Results).}

\subsection{Deployable symmetry-coset conditioning}
The non-reference relative rotations fall into a discrete set of cosets; $R'_m$ equals the rotation
part of $h=g_mg_0^{-1}$, assigned from crystallography by matching $c_m$ against $W_hc_0+t_h$
(minimum image) over the space group's operations from pymatgen---never from the observed rotation.
This yields 238 leak-free operation-cosets (vs 707 for a target-clustered codebook), available at
sampling from a symmetry template as in atom-site
generators\cite{jiao2024diffcsppp,wyckoffdiff2025}. The coset enters as a per-molecule embedding,
threaded through training and the sampler.

\subsection{$\mathrm{SO}(3)$-averaged objective}
Following the $\mathrm{SO}(3)$-averaged flow objective for conformer
generation\cite{cao2025so3averaged}, the orientation residual is averaged over $K$ prior draws per
molecule, reducing target variance at fixed data.

\subsection{Template-free coset prediction}
A packing-only classifier predicts the coset from conformer, centroids, lattice, and space group
(no orientation, so leak-free). Because its argmax is noisy, we \emph{marginalize the top-$k$}:
condition the flow on each of the $k$ most-likely cosets and select the best draw (in deployment,
by $R_{wp}$/energy; here, an oracle upper bound).

\subsection{The coset makes orientation work end-to-end}\label{sec:e2e}
Sampling all three manifolds from the prior (full de-novo generation), supplying the coset at
generation collapses the median orientation error from $93.5^\circ$ (same model, coset withheld)
to $13.4^\circ$---a $7\times$ reduction---while the centroid error is unchanged ($0.346$ vs
$0.345$; the coset does not touch it) and neither reaches exact match ($0\%$; lattice $0.44$ /
centroid $0.35$ dominate). The coset thus makes the orientation component work end-to-end; joint
lattice--centroid generation is the remaining exact-match bottleneck.

\subsection{Orientation-isolated reconstruction}
With the true packing supplied and only $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ sampled, the coset-conditioned flow
reconstructs $27.5\%$ of held-out packings (best-of-8, up to $37.4\%$ at looser oracle-validated
tolerances), median orientation error $41.1^\circ$; oracle $100\%$.

\subsection{Template-free deployment via top-$k$ marginalization}\label{sec:free}
The packing-only predictor is $39.5\%$ top-1 ($4\times$ the $10.3\%$ majority), but the true coset
is in its top-3 for $75.8\%$ of molecules, top-5 for $85.6\%$, top-10 for $90.9\%$
(Table~\ref{tab:cover}). The signal is present; the argmax merely cannot commit. Marginalizing the
top-5 pulls the predicted-coset orientation error from $64.8^\circ$ (top-1) to $51.2^\circ$, toward
the $41.1^\circ$ template ceiling. At the larger corpus (\S\ref{sec:scale}) the predictor sharpens
only to $43.1\%$ top-1 and its predicted cosets still reconstruct well below template grade, so
closing this residual---a sharper predictor or per-molecule combinatorial search---remains open.

\subsection{Scale}\label{sec:scale}
Retraining the full pipeline on a $2{,}539$-crystal CSD corpus ($2{,}464$ multi-copy after
re-gauge; $2{,}168$ train / $296$ validation; $320$ distinct operation-cosets), roughly
$2.2\times$ the diagnostic set, sharpens every orientation metric while leaving the packing
bottleneck untouched (Table~\ref{tab:scale}). The deployable-coset gate does not merely hold: the
non-reference loss drop rises to $54.3\pm0.8\%$ (3 seeds; $53.5/54.4/55.1\%$) against a paired
no-coset control at $36.8\pm2.1\%$ ($35.2/36.0/39.1\%$), \emph{widening} the coset advantage from
$+13.6$ to $+17.5$ percentage points---the benefit is a property of the factorization, not a
small-data artifact. End-to-end with the coset supplied, the median orientation error falls to
$4.3^\circ$ (from $13.4^\circ$ at diagnostic scale), and orientation-isolated reconstruction
climbs to $56.8\%$ best-of-8 at $31.7^\circ$ (from $27.5\%$ at $41.1^\circ$). Exact end-to-end
match remains $0\%$ with the lattice ($0.42$) and centroid ($0.35$) component errors essentially
unchanged: scaling data sharpens orientation but does not move the joint lattice--centroid
bottleneck, cleanly isolating where the remaining work lies. The packing-only predictor sharpens
only modestly (top-1 $43.1\%$ vs.\ $39.5\%$; majority $10.4\%$), and its predicted cosets do not
yet reconstruct at template grade---top-5 marginalization reaches $6.1\%$ best-of-8 at $44.0^\circ$
against $56.8\%$/$31.7^\circ$ with the true template---so a sharper predictor or per-molecule
combinatorial search over the template-free path remains the open problem.

\subsection{Comparison to rigid-body baselines}\label{sec:bench}
To make the contribution legible to the same reviewers, we re-score held-out generations from the
deployable coset model ($N\!\approx\!1.1$k) in MolCrystalFlow's exact
units\cite{zeng2026molcrystalflow}: pymatgen \textsc{StructureMatcher} ($\text{ltol}{=}0.3$,
$\text{atol}{=}10^\circ$, site tolerance swept $0.5$--$1.2$), best-of-$10$ match rate, and the
relative cell-volume deviation (RMAD) over all ten samples. We report a paired de-novo pass (no
template, coset off---the MolCrystalFlow/MOFFlow regime) against our symmetry-conditioned pass
(coset on) on \emph{identical} prior draws (Table~\ref{tab:bench}).

Two readings are deliberate and honest. First, end-to-end our diagnostic-scale model is \emph{not}
competitive with the dedicated flows on either axis: best-of-$10$ match is at floor
($0$--$0.8\%$) against MolCrystalFlow's $\sim\!6.8\%$ at $\text{stol}\,0.8$, and cell-volume RMAD
($\sim\!31\%$) lands between MOFFlow ($18.8\%$) and raw Genarris-3 ($59\%$) but well above
MolCrystalFlow ($3.9\%$). This is the lattice/centroid bottleneck of Table~\ref{tab:scale}
($0\%$ exact match) now measured in the baselines' units: our lattice and centroid heads are
diagnostic-grade and we make no end-to-end generation claim. Second, and the point of the paper,
is the axis those baselines leave \emph{unconditioned}. The coset barely moves RMAD ($32.9\%\!\to\!
30.7\%$)---it conditions orientation, not packing---but on the same prior draws it collapses the
median best-of-$10$ orientation error from $103^\circ$ (de-novo) to $14^\circ$ (conditioned),
consistent with the end-to-end collapse of \S\ref{sec:scale}. MolCrystalFlow and MOFFlow are both
symmetry-free and have no such lever; MolCrystalFlow is itself only \emph{competitive} with the
space-group-conditioned Genarris-3, corroborating that symmetry conditioning---not packing---is the
remaining lever. Our contribution supplies exactly that for rigid-body orientation and composes
with the strong packing these methods already have. \emph{Caveat:} identical matcher and match@10
as MolCrystalFlow, but our corpus is a smaller CSD-derived molecular set (not Th\"urlemann/OMC25) at
diagnostic scale---a comparable-task alignment, not a strict head-to-head.

lattice/centroid-limited, and template-free reconstruction is not yet at the template ceiling.

\section{Conclusions}
% =====================================================================
The learnable content of a rigid-body orientation field is the space-group-determined relative
rotation; a deployable, leak-free coset label delivers it, an $\mathrm{SO}(3)$-averaged objective
reaches the oracle codebook, the coset makes orientation work end-to-end, and top-$k$
marginalization opens a template-free path. This gives rigid-body molecular-crystal
generators\cite{zeng2026molcrystalflow} a concrete recipe: condition orientation on the generating
coset and sample only the free pose.
