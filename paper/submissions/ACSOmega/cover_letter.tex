Research Article in \textit{ACS Omega}.

In the interest of full transparency: this work was previously submitted to the
\textit{Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling} (manuscript ID
\textbf{ci-2026-02477r}), where it was declined without external review on
28 July 2026. The Associate Editor kindly offered a transfer to \textit{ACS

reviewer recommendations to respond to.

Rigid-body flow matching has become an attractive route to molecular-crystal
structure prediction: freezing each molecule's conformer reduces the problem to a
lattice, a set of fractional centroids, and a per-molecule orientation on SO(3), a
low-dimensional target that fast flow-matching samplers solve in tens of steps.
Yet the current generation of rigid-body molecular-crystal generators leaves
crystallographic symmetry unconditioned on the orientation degree of freedom, and
symmetry- or Wyckoff-conditioned generation has to date been demonstrated only for
inorganic atomic sites. This manuscript closes that gap and makes the conditioning
deployable.

The contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A mechanism, then a method.} The per-molecule orientation target
factorizes into a space-group-determined relative rotation, which a flow learns,
and a gauge-free asymmetric-unit pose, which it cannot regress from packing. This
explains why an unconditioned orientation flow stalls, and it identifies exactly
what to condition on.

\item \textbf{Deployable symmetry-coset conditioning.} A leak-free coset label ---
the generating space-group operation, recovered from centroids against a symmetry
template at sampling time, never from the observed rotation --- conditions the
orientation flow. It recovers two-thirds of an oracle-codebook benefit
($+41.1\%$ held-out non-reference orientation loss vs.\ $+27.5\%$ for a paired
control), and an SO(3)-averaged objective closes the rest ($+47.7\%$). Supplying
the coset at generation collapses the end-to-end median orientation error
sevenfold ($93.5^\circ \to 13.4^\circ$), and the advantage widens with data.

\item \textbf{The first fully symmetry-conditioned molecular-crystal flow.}
Extending the conditioning to the lattice --- a crystal-family mask on an
O(3)-invariant log-metric cell parametrization --- composes with the orientation
coset to condition every symmetry-constrained degree of freedom. Paired with an
unsupervised, symmetry-preserving packing finisher and a fully ablated stack of
levers, it lifts held-out exact match from $0\%$ to $6.9\%$ (strict best-of-10,
StructureMatcher stol 1.0) --- statistical parity with the symmetry-free
MolCrystalFlow ($\sim$8\%), from a flow that matched nothing --- and $10.7\%$ with
orientation test-time augmentation.
\end{itemize}

I believe the work fits \textit{ACS Omega}'s interdisciplinary remit. It sits at
the interface of crystallography, machine learning, and solid-state chemistry: the
result is a design rule for chemists building molecular-crystal structure
generators --- condition orientation on the generating coset and the lattice on the
crystal family, sample only the free pose, and finish with a symmetry-preserving
physical relaxation --- reported with each lever's contribution quantified rather
than as a single state-of-the-art number. The limitations are stated explicitly,
including the comparable-task (not head-to-head) nature of the MolCrystalFlow
comparison and the confidence interval on the parity claim.

The manuscript has been restructured to \textit{ACS Omega}'s section order, placing
Methods after the Conclusions. The data, analysis, and claims are unchanged from
the version \textit{JCIM} considered.
